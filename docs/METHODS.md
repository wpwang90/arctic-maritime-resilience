# Method definitions

## Physical network

Consolidate port identities before counting connections. Where several verified
entries belong to one port, select a water-connected entry appropriate to the
connection. Keep geographical access separate from economic capacity.

The baseline uses accepted actual AIS geometry when available. Documented
water-network geometry supports other records. Distances are sums of geodesic
segment lengths, not straight endpoint chords or distances in a map projection.
Land crossing, large AIS gaps and disconnected access must remain explicit.

## Arctic routes

The route bank has NWP, NEP, NSR and TSR cores. Each complete voyage includes
water-constrained access, its required northern core and egress. A common set of
model-year members must support all voyage elements. The primary six-model,
ten-year window has 60 model-year members per month, not 60 independent models.
Native-cell nearest-neighbour sampling is not downscaling.

The ice-capable screen uses concentration at most 80% and thickness at most
1.2 m. Low-concentration missing thickness and alternative screens are handled
explicitly by the climate sensitivity scripts. The primary admission threshold
is 50%; the 30/50/70/90% analysis separates admission from candidate-search
sensitivity. None of these screens certifies a vessel for Arctic operation.

## Disturbance response

Demand is a fixed directed port-pair, vessel-class and month record. The
conventional and Arctic-enabled states face the same restrictions. Conventional,
Arctic and unserved shares use the original represented trade denominator.

The primary choice uses a 5% distance-equivalent Arctic premium. This is not a
calibrated monetary freight premium. Arctic share exceeding conventional share
is an accounting crossover in the represented cohort; it is not evidence by
itself of a dynamical critical point or annual global dominance.

Four staged disturbance mechanisms are hypothetical stress tests, not
reconstructions of actual event probabilities. Dependency-guided closure search
traces newly Arctic-selected demand back to its original chokepoints. Independent
one-to-five-closure optimizations must not be interpreted as one nested sequence.
Reopening a retained gate must affect the selected routes or costs.

## Trade and national accounts

Bilateral monetary weights are allocated once across the represented ports,
ship classes and months. A route crossing multiple gates divides its weight
in the conserved Sankey; full gate exposure is a different overlapping measure.
The modal-share forecast is a separate specification and must not be confused
with an observed 2024 sea fraction.

Coastal transit importance counts represented trade on selected routes entering
a specified proximity band, excluding endpoint economies. The primary band is
100 km and is not a territorial boundary. Rank is not port revenue, sovereignty,
supplier quality or national welfare.

Direct exposure combines transport and insurance margins, delay and inventory
proxies. Commodity attribution respects observed annual country-industry
ceilings. Forward input-output extraction describes structural output exposure,
not measured GDP or an estimated equilibrium. Do not add direct transport
exposure to structural output exposure.

## Procurement experiment

The S25 market comprises 107 represented economies and 763 directed links in
four independent vessel-commodity markets. Buyers retain proportional sourcing
or move up to 20% of demand to one of up to three existing secondary suppliers.
There is no entry into an unobserved bilateral relationship.

The six arms are CC, CA, AC, AA, AO and AP. The first letter fixes conventional
or Arctic route inputs. C/A in the second position shows old/new ranks; O/P
updates only the buyer's rank or visible supplier ranks. Matched fixed-route
prompts differ only in rank information. Exporter policies are fixed within a
route state and ration submitted orders.

Two Qwen checkpoints and two prompt presentations provide four configurations.
Each uses 384 matched conditional policy draws and three market-clearing
periods. Proposed contract changes, realised leading-supplier changes and
transferred trade are separate outcomes. Import disruption burden is extra
logistics cost plus the contracting charge and the shortfall charge. Export
deliveries are reported separately, not added as welfare.

LLM responses and simulation intervals are not observations of national
behaviour. No model was calibrated to make switching rates significant.
